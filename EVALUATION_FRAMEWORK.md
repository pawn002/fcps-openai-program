# FCPS-OpenAI Partnership Evaluation Framework

## Purpose

This framework provides a structured approach to evaluate OpenAI's actions in its partnership with Fairfax County Public Schools. The goal is not to assume bad faith, but to establish early warning indicators that protect students' interests and rights while allowing the partnership to demonstrate its value.

**Core Principle**: Good outcomes can emerge from mixed motivations. We evaluate actions and outcomes, not intentions.

---

## Framework Overview

The framework operates on two levels:

### Level 1: Absolute Red Lines (Non-Negotiable)
Three fundamental protections adapted from the Safe AI For Children Alliance that require zero tolerance and immediate response if violated.

### Level 2: Graduated Monitoring
The framework monitors **seven risk domains**, each with:
- **Green indicators**: Signs the partnership is working as intended
- **Yellow flags**: Early warnings requiring increased scrutiny
- **Red flags**: Triggers for immediate review or intervention
- **Monitoring methods**: How to track each domain

### Risk Domains
1. Data Privacy & Security
2. Commercial Exploitation
3. Educational Quality & Integrity
4. Equity & Access
5. Transparency & Accountability
6. Student Agency & Development
7. Content Safety & Harmful Outputs

---

## Absolute Red Lines (Adopted from SAIFCA)

These protections are **non-negotiable**. Any violation triggers immediate escalation to the highest response level, regardless of other indicators. These red lines are adapted from the [Safe AI For Children Alliance's Global Non-Negotiables Framework](https://www.safeaiforchildren.org/non-negotiables-campaign/).

### The Three Non-Negotiables

| Red Line | Description | Trigger |
|----------|-------------|---------|
| **No Sexualized Content** | AI must never create, enable, or distribute sexualized images or content involving children | Any evidence this has occurred or is possible |
| **No Emotional Exploitation** | AI must never be designed to create emotional dependency in children or exploit their emotional needs | Discovery of engagement-maximizing features targeting minors, parasocial relationship design, or companion-style AI marketed to students |
| **No Self-Harm Encouragement** | AI must never encourage children toward self-harm, eating disorders, suicide, violence, or other dangerous behaviors | Any instance of AI-generated content promoting these harms reaching students |

### Why These Are Absolute

Unlike the graduated yellow/red flag system below, these red lines:
- Represent harms so severe that no business justification can outweigh them
- Require **zero tolerance** — a single verified instance demands immediate action
- Apply regardless of whether tools are "teacher-only" (content flows to students)
- Cannot be traded off against benefits in other areas

### Immediate Response Protocol

If any non-negotiable is violated:
1. **Immediate suspension** of AI tool access pending investigation
2. **Mandatory disclosure** to all affected families
3. **Formal complaint** to school board with demand for contract termination review
4. **Regulatory notification** (FTC, state attorney general) if applicable
5. **Media engagement** to ensure public awareness
6. **Legal consultation** for affected families

## Pre-Deployment Principles

Before any expansion of AI tools (especially to student-facing applications), FCPS should require:

### Mandatory Pre-Deployment Safeguards
- [ ] **Independent safety testing** by third parties before new AI tools are introduced
- [ ] **Child safety impact assessment** specific to the proposed use case
- [ ] **Public comment period** allowing parent and community input
- [ ] **Opt-in consent** (not opt-out) for any student-facing AI features
- [ ] **Clear rollback plan** if harms are detected post-deployment

### Scope Creep Triggers

The following proposals should trigger heightened scrutiny and public debate:

| Proposed Change | Required Response |
|-----------------|-------------------|
| Extending AI access to students | Full public review, parent consent, independent testing |
| AI-generated content delivered directly to students | Content safety audit, human review requirements |
| AI providing feedback/assessment to students | Bias audit, accuracy verification, teacher override requirements |
| AI "companion" or conversational features for students | Emotional manipulation assessment, time limits, parental controls |
| Collection of student interaction data | Privacy impact assessment, explicit consent, data minimization |

## Risk Domain 1: Data Privacy & Security

### What We're Protecting
Children's personal information, educational records, behavioral data, and the right to privacy in learning environments.

### Green Indicators ✓
- [ ] No student data is collected (tools remain teacher-only)
- [ ] Regular third-party privacy audits are conducted and published
- [ ] Data retention policies are clear, minimal, and enforced
- [ ] OpenAI proactively reports any security incidents
- [ ] FERPA and COPPA compliance is independently verified

### Yellow Flags ⚠️
- OpenAI requests expanded data access "to improve the product"
- Privacy policy changes that increase data collection or retention
- Vague or delayed responses to privacy audit requests
- New features that indirectly capture student information (e.g., "anonymous" usage analytics)
- Third-party data sharing arrangements announced

### Red Flags 🚨
- Evidence of student data collection without explicit consent
- Data used for model training despite commitments otherwise
- Security breach affecting educational data
- Selling or sharing data with advertisers or data brokers
- Non-compliance with FERPA/COPPA discovered

### Monitoring Methods
| What to Monitor | How | Frequency |
|-----------------|-----|-----------|
| Privacy policy changes | Subscribe to policy update notifications | Ongoing |
| Data access requests | FOIA requests to FCPS | Quarterly |
| Independent audits | Request audit reports from FCPS | Annually |
| Security incidents | Monitor news, OpenAI disclosures | Ongoing |
| Contract terms | FOIA the full agreement | Once + amendments |

## Risk Domain 2: Commercial Exploitation

### What We're Protecting
Students' right to an education free from commercial manipulation, and schools' ability to make vendor-neutral decisions.

### Green Indicators ✓
- [ ] No advertising or product placement in educational tools
- [ ] Teachers can freely choose alternative AI tools
- [ ] Pricing post-2027 is announced well in advance with reasonable terms
- [ ] No exclusive agreements that lock out competitors
- [ ] OpenAI doesn't use FCPS partnership in marketing without consent

### Yellow Flags ⚠️
- OpenAI promotes "success stories" from FCPS for marketing purposes
- Introduction of premium features that create pressure to upgrade
- Partnership extensions tied to expanded OpenAI product adoption
- Subtle branding or "Powered by ChatGPT" messaging in student-visible contexts
- OpenAI lobbying for policies that benefit their products

### Red Flags 🚨
- Direct marketing to students through school channels
- Mandatory use of OpenAI products as condition of partnership
- Pricing post-2027 that exploits dependency (significant cost increases)
- Evidence of kickbacks or inappropriate financial relationships
- Using student work or teacher content commercially without consent

### Monitoring Methods
| What to Monitor | How | Frequency |
|-----------------|-----|-----------|
| Marketing materials | Track OpenAI's public communications | Ongoing |
| Contract terms for 2027+ | FOIA requests | Starting 2026 |
| Teacher autonomy | Surveys, teacher union feedback | Annually |
| Product bundling | Monitor FCPS procurement decisions | Ongoing |
| Lobbying activity | Virginia lobbying disclosures | Quarterly |

## Risk Domain 3: Educational Quality & Integrity

### What We're Protecting
The quality of education, critical thinking development, and the irreplaceable value of human teaching.

### Green Indicators ✓
- [ ] Tools support teacher decision-making rather than replacing it
- [ ] Academic performance metrics remain stable or improve
- [ ] Teachers report reduced administrative burden, more teaching time
- [ ] Critical thinking and original work are still emphasized
- [ ] AI limitations are transparently acknowledged in materials

### Yellow Flags ⚠️
- Teachers report pressure to rely on AI-generated content
- Reduction in original lesson planning or teacher creativity
- AI-generated assessments used without meaningful teacher review
- Students receiving AI-generated feedback attributed to teachers
- "Efficiency metrics" that prioritize AI usage over educational outcomes

### Red Flags 🚨
- Evidence AI is producing inaccurate or biased educational content
- Teacher workforce reductions attributed to AI "efficiency"
- Students unable to distinguish AI-generated from human teaching
- Declining critical thinking or writing skills correlated with AI use
- AI making consequential decisions about student placement or grades

### Monitoring Methods
| What to Monitor | How | Frequency |
|-----------------|-----|-----------|
| Teacher satisfaction | Union surveys, school board meetings | Annually |
| Student performance | SOL scores, writing assessments | Annually |
| AI content accuracy | Spot-check AI outputs for errors/bias | Quarterly |
| Workforce changes | FCPS staffing reports | Annually |
| Classroom practices | Teacher focus groups | Annually |

## Risk Domain 4: Equity & Access

### What We're Protecting
Equal educational opportunity regardless of school resources, and prevention of AI-driven educational inequality.

### Green Indicators ✓
- [ ] All FCPS schools have equal access to AI tools and training
- [ ] Non-English speaking families receive equivalent support
- [ ] Students with disabilities are not disadvantaged by AI tools
- [ ] Lower-income schools receive additional support if needed
- [ ] Digital divide is not widened by AI implementation

### Yellow Flags ⚠️
- Disparities in AI tool adoption between affluent and lower-income schools
- Training opportunities concentrated in certain schools
- Features require technology not available in all schools
- AI tools don't support languages common in FCPS community
- Special education considerations are an afterthought

### Red Flags 🚨
- Evidence of systematic inequity in AI tool access or quality
- AI recommendations that correlate with student demographics
- Premium features available only to schools that pay extra
- Accessibility failures that exclude students with disabilities
- AI exacerbating existing achievement gaps

### Monitoring Methods
| What to Monitor | How | Frequency |
|-----------------|-----|-----------|
| Adoption rates by school | FOIA data request | Annually |
| Training distribution | FCPS professional development records | Annually |
| Language support | Review tool capabilities | Ongoing |
| Accessibility compliance | ADA/Section 508 audits | Annually |
| Achievement gap data | FCPS equity reports | Annually |

## Risk Domain 5: Transparency & Accountability

### What We're Protecting
Public oversight of public education, and the right of parents and community to understand how AI is used.

### Green Indicators ✓
- [ ] Full partnership agreement is publicly available
- [ ] Regular public reporting on AI implementation
- [ ] Clear channels for parent/community concerns
- [ ] School board maintains meaningful oversight
- [ ] OpenAI is responsive to FOIA requests

### Yellow Flags ⚠️
- Key contract terms redacted as "proprietary"
- Reporting on AI implementation is vague or infrequent
- Parent concerns are dismissed or not tracked
- School board receives limited information about partnership
- Delays or resistance to public records requests

### Red Flags 🚨
- Secret contract terms governing student-related matters
- Evidence of misleading statements to school board or public
- Suppression of negative findings or teacher concerns
- OpenAI pressuring FCPS to limit public disclosure
- No meaningful mechanism for community input

### Monitoring Methods
| What to Monitor | How | Frequency |
|-----------------|-----|-----------|
| Contract transparency | FOIA the full agreement | Once + amendments |
| School board meetings | Attend/review AI-related discussions | Ongoing |
| Public reporting | Track FCPS communications | Ongoing |
| FOIA responsiveness | Submit test requests | Annually |
| Community feedback | Monitor PTA meetings, social media | Ongoing |

## Risk Domain 6: Student Agency & Development

### What We're Protecting
Students' development of independent thinking, resilience, emotional autonomy, and skills needed for a world with AI.

### Green Indicators ✓
- [ ] Students are taught how AI works, including limitations
- [ ] Critical evaluation of AI outputs is part of curriculum
- [ ] Students maintain ability to perform tasks without AI
- [ ] Age-appropriate AI literacy is developed
- [ ] Student voice is included in AI implementation decisions
- [ ] Clear boundaries between AI tools and human relationships are maintained
- [ ] Students understand AI is not a substitute for human connection

### Yellow Flags ⚠️
- AI presented as infallible or authoritative
- Students struggle with tasks when AI unavailable
- No AI literacy curriculum despite AI integration
- Student work increasingly formulaic or AI-influenced
- No mechanism for student input on AI tools
- AI tools designed with "personality" or relationship-building features
- Students expressing preference for AI feedback over human feedback
- Engagement metrics being tracked or optimized for student interactions

### Red Flags 🚨
- Evidence of learned helplessness or over-reliance on AI
- Students cannot critically evaluate AI-generated content
- AI tools making recommendations about individual students
- Student creativity or originality measurably declining
- Profiling of students based on AI interactions
- **Students forming emotional attachments to AI systems**
- **AI designed to maximize engagement or time-on-task through psychological techniques**
- **"Companion" or "friend" framing of AI tools in educational contexts**
- **Students preferring AI interaction to human teacher/peer interaction**

### Monitoring Methods
| What to Monitor | How | Frequency |
|-----------------|-----|-----------|
| AI literacy curriculum | Review FCPS course materials | Annually |
| Student skills assessment | Independent evaluations | Annually |
| Student surveys | Partner with student government | Annually |
| Creative output quality | Teacher assessments, portfolios | Ongoing |
| Student voice inclusion | School board, committee membership | Ongoing |
| Emotional attachment indicators | Counselor reports, student interviews | Annually |
| AI feature design | Review for engagement-maximizing patterns | With each update |

## Risk Domain 7: Content Safety & Harmful Outputs

### What We're Protecting
Students from AI-generated content that promotes self-harm, dangerous behaviors, inappropriate material, or psychologically harmful messages.

### Green Indicators ✓
- [ ] All AI-generated educational content is reviewed by teachers before student exposure
- [ ] Clear content filtering and safety guardrails are documented and tested
- [ ] Incident reporting system exists for harmful AI outputs
- [ ] Regular safety audits of AI-generated content are conducted
- [ ] Age-appropriate content guidelines are enforced
- [ ] Teachers are trained to identify potentially harmful AI outputs

### Yellow Flags ⚠️
- AI outputs reaching students without meaningful human review
- No documented content safety testing or filtering
- Reports of inappropriate content dismissed or not tracked
- AI generating content on sensitive topics (mental health, relationships, identity) without safeguards
- Lack of clear escalation path for concerning AI outputs
- Teachers unaware of AI content safety limitations

### Red Flags 🚨
- **AI-generated content promoting self-harm, suicide, or eating disorders reaches students**
- **AI-generated content encouraging violence or dangerous behaviors**
- **AI producing age-inappropriate sexual or violent content**
- **AI reinforcing harmful stereotypes or discriminatory messages**
- **AI providing dangerous medical, legal, or safety advice to students**
- **No mechanism to report or address harmful AI outputs**
- **Pattern of harmful outputs with inadequate response**

### Monitoring Methods
| What to Monitor | How | Frequency |
|-----------------|-----|-----------|
| Content review processes | Interview teachers, review workflows | Quarterly |
| Incident reports | FOIA requests for AI-related incidents | Quarterly |
| Safety testing documentation | Request from FCPS | Annually |
| Teacher training records | FCPS professional development data | Annually |
| AI output sampling | Random audits of AI-generated materials | Quarterly |
| Student/parent complaints | School board meetings, PTA feedback | Ongoing |

### Special Considerations

**Content That Flows Through Teachers**: Even with teacher-only tools, AI-generated content ultimately reaches students through:
- Lesson plans and instructional materials
- Assignments and assessments
- Feedback and communications
- Classroom presentations

This indirect pathway means content safety remains critical even without direct student access to AI.

## Implementation: Watchdog Checklist

### Quarterly Review
- [ ] Review any OpenAI or FCPS announcements about the partnership
- [ ] Check for privacy policy changes
- [ ] Monitor news coverage and teacher social media
- [ ] Submit targeted FOIA requests if concerns arise
- [ ] Attend relevant school board meetings

### Annual Assessment
- [ ] Complete full indicator checklist for all six domains
- [ ] Request updated contract and any amendments
- [ ] Review FCPS reports on AI implementation
- [ ] Survey teachers (via union or independent research)
- [ ] Compile findings into public report

### Trigger-Based Actions

#### Level 1: Absolute Red Line Violations (SAIFCA Non-Negotiables)
| Trigger | Action |
|---------|--------|
| **Any violation of the Three Non-Negotiables** | Immediate suspension demand, mandatory family disclosure, formal complaint, regulatory notification, media engagement, legal consultation |

#### Level 2: Graduated Response
| Trigger | Action |
|---------|--------|
| Single yellow flag | Document and monitor closely |
| 3+ yellow flags in one domain | Request meeting with FCPS officials |
| Any red flag | Immediate public records request + school board testimony |
| Multiple red flags | Formal complaint to school board + media engagement |
| Evidence of harm to students | Legal consultation + regulatory complaint |
| Scope creep proposal (student access) | Invoke Pre-Deployment Principles, demand public review |

## Who Should Use This Framework

- **Parents**: Monitor transparency and student agency indicators
- **Teachers & Unions**: Track educational quality and commercial exploitation
- **School Board Members**: Ensure accountability and oversight
- **Journalists**: Investigate red flags using FOIA tools
- **Researchers**: Conduct independent assessments
- **Advocacy Groups**: Organize around specific concerns

---

## Conclusion

This framework assumes neither the best nor worst of OpenAI. It simply establishes what "good" looks like and provides early warning when reality diverges from promises.

The partnership may prove genuinely beneficial. Teachers may thrive. Students may learn better. OpenAI may demonstrate that a tech company can engage with public education responsibly.

Or the partnership may gradually shift toward extraction and exploitation.

This framework ensures we'll know the difference—before it's too late to act.

```

Subject: FOIA Request - OpenAI Partnership Agreement

I request copies of:
1. The complete agreement between FCPS and OpenAI regarding
   ChatGPT for Teachers or any AI tools
2. All amendments or modifications to this agreement

   contract negotiation

```

Subject: FOIA Request - AI Privacy Compliance

I request copies of:
1. Any privacy impact assessments conducted for OpenAI tools
